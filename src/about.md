## How I got here

My first job in software was an internship at Thomson Reuters in 2007, working on the indexing part of a legal search engine. After that I spent more than ten years writing backend software in China: credit rating models for big banks at Moody's Analytics, bond trading systems at Ping An Securities, and internal systems at S.F. Express, one of China's largest delivery companies.

The industries changed, but the job stayed the same: take something people did slowly or by hand, and turn it into software they could trust. At Ping An, traders priced bonds in Excel; I turned those spreadsheets into production services. At Moody's, a tool for viewing rating models took hours to open. The cause was a brute-force cycle check, and replacing it with a topological sort brought the wait down to seconds.

## What I'm doing now

After moving to Canada, I went back to school at Sheridan College for two graduate certificates, one in IoT and machine intelligence and one in advanced game programming. I then joined an applied research team at Sheridan, first as a research assistant and now as a machine learning specialist. Today I'm teaching computers to spot defects in inspection photos.

In my own time I build things to learn how they work: a Rails app for law firms whose busiest query I made about 900× faster, a 3D renderer in C++ and OpenGL, a desktop app for practising English listening and speaking, and a few games you can play in the browser.

## How I work

- **I fix the cause, not the symptom.** A quick patch feels good today and costs twice as much next month.
- **I measure.** If I say something got faster, there is a before and an after number next to it.
- **I use AI every day.** Claude Code is my pair programmer, and every change still goes through my review, tests and CI before it merges.
- **I like teaching.** At Moody's and S.F. Express I walked new engineers through the business and the code, and reviewed their work until they could ship on their own.
